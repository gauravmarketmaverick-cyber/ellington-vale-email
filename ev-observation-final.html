Shivani — we only write these for people whose work is already ahead of how it appears. That is the case here, which is the only reason this is worth your two minutes.

 

What a stranger sees first

Before a meeting, before a reply, this is what someone finds when they search her name. Four empty tiles beside the one photograph the platform already compresses to a coin.

 

Observation one  —  the headline and the About

Read the two side by side. One of them could only be hers. The other could belong to almost anyone in the field.

The headline, as written

"Digital Content Specialist @ McCormick & Company | Ex: LinkedIn | Global Social Media & Content Strategy"

The About section, as written

"I'm someone who finds genuine joy in the way a well-crafted message can connect with the right people at the right time..."

The headline is specific, rare, and immediately credible. The About opens with a sentiment that a thousand other profiles in the field also open with. A reader arrives already convinced by the first line, and then spends the next four sentences waiting for the proof that earned it — proof the headline already gave them, three lines above.

 

Observation two  —  the register

Three tones currently share one page: a motivational banner phrase, a warm candid photograph, and a precise senior title. Each is fine alone. Together, a reader has to decide which one to believe — and usually believes the quietest of the three, which is rarely the title she has actually earned.

 

Not the photo alone — the whole page

Banner, photograph and register, changed together. Every word of the headline stays exactly as she wrote it.

As it stands

As it could be

 

What direction actually looks like

Light, posture, wardrobe, background — each one a decision, each sending a specific signal before a word is read. Four directions from one sitting, built to say four different things.

Open credibility

Soft light, a warm neutral ground, an unguarded smile. Competent without reading as cold — the photograph for a first introduction.

Set the terms

Seated, structured, unhurried. An open posture that still holds the room — the register for a founder's note or a keynote bio.

The decision-maker

Crossed arms, direct gaze, a stark ground. The most authoritative frame in the set — spend it once, not everywhere.

The strategist

Tailoring with a tie, a considering hand, warm tone underneath. Earned authority, not asserted authority.

 

And what we would not use

This one is warm and entirely true to her — worth keeping, not worth leading with. It belongs to a dinner with friends, not to the page a stranger meets before a meeting.

Genuine. Not directed. Right for a different room.

 

None of this is about vanity, and none of it is a small complaint about one photograph. It is that a genuinely rare record — LinkedIn, then global content strategy for a company most people have heard of — is currently being carried by a page that undersells it in four separate places at once. The potential is not in question. The page has simply not caught up to it yet.

 

Is that a fair reading?

If it is, an hour would be worth having. If it is not, tell us where we have it wrong — that is useful to us either way.

Take the hour

Or simply reply. One line is enough.
